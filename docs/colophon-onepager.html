Colophon

Written over, never erased.

A method for recording, measuring and disclosing the human and AI contribution while a text is being written — and publishing a record a reader can check, instead of a label they have to believe.

The problem

There are two ways to say how a text was made, and both fail

One asks a machine to guess after the fact. The other asks the author to promise. Neither survives the case that actually matters: a text written by a person and a model together.

39%

Detector accuracy on AI text edited by hand — 26% once it is paraphrased, and below chance at word level on genuinely co-written text. The difficulty peaks at roughly half AI contribution: the commonest case is the worst one.

0.1%

Share of papers that disclose AI use, across more than 164,000 — while 70% of journals have a policy requiring it. And the objection of principle stands: a text cannot prove its own authorship.

The third way

Capture the process while it happens

A colophon is the note that has closed books since the fifteenth century, declaring how they were made: printer, typeface, paper, date. This moves that function from the book to the text written together with a language model.

The payoff comes from the palimpsest — the reused manuscript in which the earlier writing stays visible beneath the new. That is also the formal property of the register: append-only, no event overwritten, every revision added rather than substituted.

The measurement

Two axes, because they are two different questions

Who wrote the words and whose content is it have different answers, and the distance between them is the most useful thing the method produces. The point below is a real one: 337 measured words, annotated span by span.

The record

One JSON line per event, each carrying the last one's fingerprint

Altering a past event invalidates every hash that follows it. The register is the evidence, and it is never edited — a correction is a new event, and the old one stays where it was.

The seal

Who, and when — each from a source that does not depend on the others

who

Ed25519 signature

Detached, verified against a key published on a domain the author controls — never one that lives inside the folder it authenticates.

when

RFC 3161 timestamp

A timestamp authority attests the register existed in that form on that date. Verifiable against a certificate bundle a reader already has.

when, again

Bitcoin anchor

Submitted to OpenTimestamps. Submitted is not anchored: it proves nothing until the calendars confirm, and the method says so rather than implying otherwise.

What it does not prove

The chain proves an event was not altered after it was recorded. It does not prove the register is complete — no voluntary system can. An author may record faithfully or may omit, and cryptography does not tell the two apart.

It is said first because it is the strongest criticism that can be made of a voluntary disclosure, and it is a fair one. The register is also compiled by the model about its own contribution: that is the structural weak point, and it is written down rather than hidden.

The value is not in the proof. It is in the responsibility an author takes on by publishing the record, and in the fact that it can be inspected.
